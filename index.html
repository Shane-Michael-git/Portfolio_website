Decision Making

Decision making can be tough to do in any context, and engineering is no exception to the rule. The inherent desires and biases of the individual engineer can cloud judgement and can ensure a less well suitied solution is chosen. Decision matrices are a logical way to break down the decision based off which iteration provides the best fit for your criteria. Each criteria is weighted, to ensure that more important criteria have a larger impact on the totals, while less important criteria will still be impactful, but will realistically represent a much smaller importance to the consumer in the final calculations. In the decision matrix below the Order is Dovetail Modular(Image 1), Cantilever Tiered(Image 2), Sealed Dry Vault(Image 3).

Dovetail Modular

This design focuses on scalability above all else. While still being highly rated on all other criteria, no other design is quite so scalable due to the modular nature. The modules slide together with a dovetail joint, and a replaceable plastic cantilever push lever that allows it to snap in and out of place quickly, and also accounts for wear and tear on moving/high stress parts.

Cantilever Tiered

This design is based on a fishermans tackle box, with multiple tiers for bulk cards held below. Likely to use cards in middle tier, and highly used cards up top. this design is much bigger, and can fit large collections, but it is much less scalable.

Sealed Dry-Vault

This design focuses on durability and card safety overall. The sealed lid and rugged design make it a powerhouse for protecting valuable cards. This design is not as scalable and is much more akin to a regular toploading card storage box.

Using the decision matrix allowed me to more accurately adhere to what consumers would want rather than going with my gut. The matrix showed how the differing specializations of the choices did actually drastically effect which would be the better design choice for our consumer base. While the Cantilever Tiered and the Sealed dry vault had their own upsides, the Dovetail Modular design better met the criteria consumers valued. This allowed me to choose this design. Furthermore I was able to use another decision matrix to decide the best kind of locking mechanism for the modular design. I was originally between a dial knob that would lock them into place after sliding, or a snap in place lock that used a button push. the dial would be replaceable, but more work to include, the button would be integrated but would deal with high stress and repeated stress, so breaking threatens to make the unit unscalable. A third contender was added, a replaceable cantilever button system, and when put against the other two in a decision matrix, blew them out of the water. So decison matrices have many uses in the product produciton pipeline.
